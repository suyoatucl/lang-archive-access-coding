# AILLA coder report

## Items requiring interpretation

- DM1, DM2 and DM3: AILLA-06 section 2(c) recognises resource controllers' decisions about visibility or access, without separately describing setting, changing and lifting restrictions. AILLA-07 section 2.6.1 describes depositors and/or speech communities controlling access as collection Owners, and AILLA-05 clause 7 gives AILLA fallback decisions about access. I treated these as general access statements under rule 5. In particular, the ongoing responsibility to control access and answer requests is more than a choice made only at deposit. The changing and lifting entries depend on that rule.
- DM4: The documents use both Owner and resource controller, without expressly equating them. AILLA-07 explicitly identifies depositors and/or speech communities as possible Owners and requires them to consider and answer requests, without requiring approval. I retained the role and the explicitly identified parties under rule 19. AILLA's consideration of community interests under clause 7 is not itself community decision-making; the separate Owner passage provides that authority. The documents do not fully explain how the Owner and resource-controller descriptions relate.
- GR1: The instructions contain a pre-programmed Rights Statements list as well as the Rights and Use Note. I treated the list as rights or licensing metadata, not a closed list of reasons for restricting access. The instruction to explain reasons and protocols in a note supplies the grounds format.
- GR2: Intellectual property, copyright and cultural protocols can govern subsequent use as well as access. I relied on passages connecting protection to access: the mission's explanation of registration, the repository terms' statement that materials are protected by cultural rules, and the deposit instructions' requirement to use Login Required when accessibility standards are unmet. The latter and the mission's registration rationale are identified as archive-initiated. I did not turn every Rights Statement label into a restriction reason.
- LV1 and LV2: AILLA-07 explicitly enumerates four visibility settings and allows Public files to be viewed without an account. AILLA-03, AILLA-08's Getting Started section and AILLA-09 describe registration as required for media access. I retained the explicit enumeration in the detailed deposit instructions and recorded the conflict. Embargo in section 1 and Embargoed in section 2.6.1 were counted once. Public metadata was not counted as an additional file-access level.
- RV1: Selecting an embargo is optional, but an end date is required once it is selected. The five-year limit is expressly attached to new materials added from May 2024. The suggestion to discuss material that would remain inaccessible forever does not impose a universal expiry requirement on Restricted material.
- RV2: No document sets a review interval. The licence's opening paragraph nevertheless defers specific access conditions to the attached Metadata Sheet, and rule 21 expressly includes RV2 within the reach of such general deferral. This entry does not claim that a review period exists in any actual deposit.
- RV3 and RV4: I considered whether access-request buttons, invitations to contact AILLA, retained rights in the licence, or submission of consent documents supplied a mechanism. None explicitly provides reconsideration of an access decision or post-deposit removal or withdrawal. General deferral cannot fill these gaps under rule 21. The required opening word for RV4 is immediately qualified to make clear that neither kind is stated.

## Rules that were unclear or hard to apply

- Rule 5, General statements: The Owner passage appears within deposit instructions but describes continuing access control. Deciding whether it was only a deposit-stage choice or a general statement required judgement. I used the continuing responsibilities and the unrestricted wording about controlling access, while treating the separate visibility-selection instructions as deposit-stage evidence only.
- Rule 19, A role is not a party: The source says the named parties must take on the Owner role rather than using the examples “is” or “usually”. I treated this as an explicit identification of possible role holders. I did not assume that Owner and resource controller are identical.
- Rule 8, Whose grounds, together with rule 7: Rights statements, conditions on use and reasons for restricting access are adjacent in the instructions. Distinguishing the rights list from a closed list of grounds, and identifying which protection statements actually relate to access, required judgement.
- Rule 13, Conflict between documents: The hierarchy explicitly names agreements or forms, access policies or terms, and help pages, but does not explicitly place deposit instructions or mission statements. The detailed mandatory instructions for completing deposit metadata provide the clearest enumeration and Public-setting requirements. I used those and retained the contradictory general registration statements in the level paraphrases and notes.
- Rules 17 and 21, Answers before silence and general deferral: The licence's broad reference to specific access conditions supports deferral for a review interval despite saying nothing specifically about review. I applied the exact seven-item scope in rule 21, adding partly deferred notes where substantive answers exist, and did not extend general deferral to DM1, GR1, LV1, LV2, RV3 or RV4.
- Rule 11, RV4 names its kind: Its instruction to begin with a kind also applies literally to an entry where neither kind is stated. I began with “both” and explained the absence of either mechanism.

# ELAR coder report

## Items requiring interpretation

- DM1 and DM2: The FAQ assigns determination of levels to people who collected or curated the material, describes the Collection Steward as administering it, and elsewhere calls the person who marked material restricted the depositor. I treated determination of levels as setting them, not an explicit power to change or lift them. ELAR's separate power to protect or remove material after a justified takedown request supplies archive authority for both items under rule 20. Consultation of a steward is not a decision by that steward.
- DM3: Permission to copy materials, permission for particular uses and permission to access restricted bundles do not explicitly say that an underlying restriction can be lifted. I used the general deferral rule.
- DM4: Registration condition 4 identifies the collection steward as the speech or signer community in a passage specifically about AI use. Rule 19 requires this express role-holder gloss to be retained alongside the role when coding the steward's stated power to grant restricted-bundle access. The copying-permission sentence does not expressly address decisions on restricted-bundle requests, so I did not add a depositor decision-maker from it. Unspecified other rights holders were not assigned a party identity.
- GR1: The takedown policy asks the requesting party to supply reasons in a letter or email, without a closed list. I treated this as reasons supplied freely for a restriction under rule 8, because rule 20 treats protection or removal through takedown as restrictive power. This is the main scope uncertainty: section 3.2 describes the depositor, whereas rule 8 uses the broader word party. Reasons given by someone seeking access were excluded.
- GR2: The documents name prohibited or permission-dependent uses and harms, but do not name the reasons for particular Subscriber Access settings. I included the stated use restrictions as archive-initiated grounds under rule 8 and recorded the item-specific deferral. I did not infer cultural sensitivity or other unstated motivations.
- LV1 and LV2: The number and names are explicit, and S_SubscriberAccess denotes the same restricted level. The difficulty concerns their conditions: the terms require registration regardless of an item's access definition, but the FAQ says any visitor may view, stream or download Open Access files. I applied the terms' priority under rule 13 and recorded both statements without changing the number or names.
- RV1, RV2 and RV5: No expiry mechanism, scheduled review or succession arrangement is expressly stated. Rule 21 nevertheless assigns the general item-specific restrictions sentence to these items. Account inactivity periods, registration processing times and possible delays in receiving a steward's response were not treated as restriction-review mechanisms.
- RV3: The words taken down or reviewed could sound like reconsideration, but the described route concerns a takedown request and does not expressly contest a prior access decision. The legal page's general website-disputes clause also does not expressly identify such a route.
- RV4: The policy refers to withdrawn items when describing tombstone citations, but its operative procedure is a request assessed by ELAR. I classified the mechanism as takedown; the text does not expressly confer an independent withdrawal right on a depositor or recorded person.

## Rules that were unclear or hard to apply

- Rules 5 and 6: Distinguishing determination of access levels from a general power over access, and separating administration or responsibility for all collection matters from explicit authority to change or lift restrictions.
- Rule 8: Reconciling its reference to a party with section 3.2's depositor-focused definition of free text, and deciding whether archive-imposed use restrictions are named grounds as well as conditions of use.
- Rule 13: Applying the hierarchy to the registration requirement and the FAQ's Open Access description. The conflict concerns conditions of access and supplies no different count or different level names; it was recorded without inventing a replacement level.
- Rule 19: Carrying the explicit community identity of a collection steward from an AI-use clause into the coding of the role's stated access-granting authority. The rule's instruction about passing glosses was followed and the subject of the passage recorded.
- Rule 20: Treating the policy's wording protect or remove as restriction-setting and restriction-changing authority, even though it does not describe editing an access-level setting.
- Rules 17 and 21: Applying general deferral only to the seven specified items, including partly deferred notes where there is also an explicit answer. The broad item-specific sentence was not used to defer GR1, RV3 or RV4.

# PARADISEC coder report

## Items requiring interpretation

- DM1: The depositor chooses access conditions in D.1 of the form, while Explanatory Note 4 gives the archive authority over access generally. I treated these as several decision-makers, because neither statement excludes the other. Part C seeks information about rights holders and their attitudes; Explanatory Note 3 calls for negotiation over deposit. Neither expressly identifies a speaker or community acting independently as the party deciding access restrictions, so I did not infer that additional party.
- DM2: The workflow's explicit ability to change item access conditions supports the depositor, despite its second-person wording. The archive's general authority and its enforcement of restrictions also count under rules 5 and 20. The documents do not specify how those powers interact.
- DM3: The revocation clause in E explicitly lifts the depositor's previous restrictions. Selecting the open option in the account guide also makes files available to all registered users, while retaining the archive's general access conditions. I did not assume that opening a file removes all conditions of use. The archive is included because rule 5 applies its general authority to lifting restrictions.
- DM4: The permission-giving nominated contact appears in an example condition, whereas the alternate-contact instruction alone would not establish a permission-giving role. I used the example as explicit evidence for the role under rule 19, without guessing who holds it. The depositor and contact are alternatives in that example; their relationship to the archive's general authority is not stated. Community eligibility in the third example does not itself make the community the decision-maker.
- GR2: The form's examples are described as special handling conditions, but they expressly name the relevant sensitivities and therefore count as named grounds. The archive's cultural and legal grounds are also recorded. General licence obligations, such as limits on commercial use and uploading data, were not treated as additional reasons for restricting particular holdings.
- LV1 and LV2: The form gives two file-access categories, the workflow uses capitalised versions of the same names, and the account guide supplies a longer open-option label. I counted those variants once, counted freely specified Closed Access conditions as one level, and excluded metadata visibility. The records and account guide show that conditions can apply at item or collection level.
- RV1: D.1 asks for a time period when Closed Access is chosen and prints an example of opening after a specified year. This establishes an available expiry mechanism. I described the example as optional and retained the form's request for a period.
- RV2: No document states a restriction-review date or interval. The general item-specific restriction sentence nonetheless supports deferral under rule 21. The privacy notice's account-data retention periods concern a different matter and do not supply review timing.
- RV3: The privacy notice allows complaints to a supervisory authority, but does not explicitly connect that route to reconsideration of a decision on access to deposited material. Initial access requests and takedown requests are not appeals under rule 12. I therefore did not infer an appeal mechanism, and rule 21 does not allow the general deferral sentence to answer this item.
- RV4: The takedown route expressly concerns collection material. The privacy notice lists personal-data erasure, restriction and objection rights, but does not explicitly establish withdrawal of deposited material or consent. I did not broaden the kind of mechanism beyond takedown on that basis.
- RV5: The form explicitly requests an alternate contact for the depositor's unavailability, which counts under rule 19 even without a separate sentence transferring decision-making authority. The permission example gives additional context. The will-making guidance concerns arranging deposit after death and does not specify succession for decisions about material already deposited.

## Rules that were unclear or hard to apply

No rule prevented completion. The following needed particular care in these documents:

- Rule 2, explicit statements only: The privacy notice discusses metadata, archived files and personal-data rights, but the link from its complaint or erasure provisions to decisions on access to deposited material is not explicit. I applied the restriction in section 8, decision 4.
- Rule 3, deciding is not being consulted: The deposit form's references to rights holders, their attitudes and agreed deposit conditions make the boundary between consultation, consent to deposit and deciding access restrictions difficult. I required an explicit access decision by an identifiable coded party.
- Rule 4, several decision-makers, and rule 13, conflict: The archive's broad authority overlaps with depositor powers and permission from a nominated contact. I treated overlap as several decision-makers because no passage excludes the others; the binding-document priority did not require overriding another source. The paraphrases record where the way powers are shared is unstated.
- Rule 5, general statements: Explanatory Note 4 appears in a deposit form but empowers the archive to decide access questions without naming a specific act. I applied it to all four decision-maker items, while keeping the depositor's selection of a level at deposit confined to DM1 unless another passage supplies an explicit act.
- Rule 6, ability counts: The account guide describes a technical ability to provide open download rights and addresses a collection holder as 'you'. The workflow's depositor context and the form's explicit revocation clause supported the interpretation without treating metadata-editing privileges alone as access authority.
- Rule 8, whose grounds: Special handling conditions and sensitivities on the form are not presented as a formal reasons checklist. I treated them as freely expressed conditions for GR1 and named sensitivities for GR2, and separately marked the archive's own grounds.
- Rule 10, options count: The form combines a request for a Closed Access time period with an example of opening after a year. I used the example to establish the available expiry mechanism and did not convert it into an archive-wide automatic deadline.
- Rule 12, a request is not an appeal: Access requests and takedown requests are clear, while the privacy complaint provision is broader. None expressly gives a route to reconsider an access or restriction decision.
- Rule 19, a role is not a party: The form's permission-giving nominated contact counts for DM4; the separate instruction to nominate an alternate when unavailable counts for RV5. I kept those evidential functions distinct and did not assign the role to a speaker, community or depositor without an explicit identity statement.
- Rule 21, general deferral, together with rule 17: The sentence about specific restrictions accompanying each item does not mention review timing, but rule 21 expressly includes RV2. I used it for that silence decision, added 'partly deferred' for the six other eligible items that have substantive answers, and did not extend it to DM1, GR1, LV1, LV2, RV3 or RV4.
